## Development and Authorship

SOS, Carter, SIS, EAS, and their underlying governance, memory, reasoning, and deterministic-computation architectures were conceived and developed by Michael D. Lewis through a multiyear process of independent research, implementation, testing, and architectural refinement.

The systems originated as manually developed software and evolved through repeated experimentation, failure analysis, and subsystem integration. More recently, AI-assisted development tools, including OpenAI Codex, have been used to accelerate implementation, testing, documentation, refactoring, and expansion.

AI assistance does not replace the architectural authorship or engineering direction behind these systems. I define the system objectives, conceptual models, subsystem responsibilities, governance requirements, integration boundaries, acceptance criteria, and validation procedures. AI-generated contributions are reviewed, tested, corrected, and accepted or rejected under my direction.

CSC represents a more extensively AI-assisted implementation than the earlier SOS, SIS, and EAS foundations. However, its purpose, architecture, security boundaries, governance model, and integration with Carter were designed within the broader Synthetic OS architecture that I created.

Some designed CSC integrations remain planned; current implementation status is
maintained in [`docs/CSC.md`](docs/CSC.md). That distinction does not alter
Michael D. Lewis's originating architectural authorship.

I describe this work as AI-assisted engineering: human-originated architecture and technical direction, implemented through a combination of manual development and governed AI coding assistance.
